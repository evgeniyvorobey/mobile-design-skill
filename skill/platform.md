# Platform facts

Checked against Apple's and Google's documentation between 2026-10-02 and 2026-10-24. These are the facts that changed recently or are often remembered a version behind. For the versions named here, this file wins over memory. For anything not listed, use what you know and name the OS version when the answer depends on it.

## iOS and iPadOS 26 and 27

- **Liquid Glass.** System bars and controls float above content and size themselves. Use the system components and do not pin bar heights in a spec: 44 pt and 49 pt are UIKit values from before iOS 26. Small glass elements switch between a light and a dark look with the content beneath, and system label colours follow them; set text and symbols on glass in those colours and keep brand colour for one emphasised element (HIG, Color and Materials).
- **Colour variants.** Give every custom colour a light and a dark value and an Increase Contrast value for each, even in an app with one appearance (HIG, Color).
- **iPhone tab bar.** It sits at the bottom, floats, and can minimise as the person scrolls. It stays visible as people move within a section; only a modal view covers it (HIG, Tab bars). Pin a custom bottom control to the safe area, not to a bar height.
- **System containers.** Put navigation in the navigation bar (the system Back and Close, screen actions as bar items with a symbol and a title) and modal tasks in system sheets and alerts. A bar, sheet or button the app draws itself keeps none of their behaviour, and iOS keeps adding behaviour to them: Liquid Glass, scroll edge effects, and on iPhone Duo the bar column and moving off the fold. A custom Back or Close looks and acts like the system one (HIG, Toolbars).
- **iPad navigation.** The system tab bar sits near the top and can convert to a sidebar. Consider the tab bar first and a sidebar when the destinations have hierarchy. iPadOS has no navigation rail.
- **Resizing.** Since iPadOS 26 app windows resize freely in the windowed modes. On iPhone, Apple names two resizable environments: iPhone Mirroring on a Mac with macOS 27, where people resize the window freely, and iPhone Duo, where an app built with the iOS 27 SDK or later changes size as the device folds and unfolds. In both, an iPhone app can have any combination of size classes; an iPhone-only app on iPad resizes like an iPad app (WWDC26 "Modernize your UIKit app", developer page "iPhone Duo: Prepare"). From iOS 27 and iPadOS 27, `UIRequiresFullScreen` no longer opts an app built with the iOS 27 SDK out of resizing, on iPhone or iPad; the window takes its new size when the drag ends (TN3192, revised 2026-10-09). An app has to work at any width, including compact.
- **iPhone Duo** has its own section below. Read all of it for any design, adaptation or review that names the device.
- **Bottom edge.** Keep custom controls and custom gestures out of the bottom safe-area inset, where the home indicator's system swipes win.
- **Back swipe.** Since iOS 26 a pushed screen also goes back on a horizontal swipe that starts anywhere in its content, not only at the leading edge (`UINavigationController.interactiveContentPopGestureRecognizer`). Keeping a custom horizontal drag (a dial, a carousel, a swiped row) away from the edge does not avoid the conflict: say which gesture wins.
- **Before a permission alert.** A custom screen shown before a system permission alert has one button, titled like "Continue" or "Next", and that button opens the alert. It has no second action and no way to close, cancel or skip the alert, unless a legal consent needs one (HIG, Privacy). An alternative to granting, such as search in place of location, belongs on the screen before it or after the person has answered the alert.
- **Approximate location.** With Precise Location off, the position is usually within 1 to 20 km of the real one and updates at most a few times an hour (`kCLLocationAccuracyReduced`). It finds the city, not the street: do not centre a street-level map on it or rank "nearest" from it.
- **Login.** An app that offers a third-party or social login also offers an equivalent that limits data to name and email, lets the person keep their email private, and does not track for advertising without consent (App Review Guideline 4.8, revised January 2024). Sign in with Apple meets it and is no longer the only way.
- **Type.** Map every text role to a Dynamic Type style. A custom face takes the style's scaling and supplies its own tracking. At large text sizes, controls side by side stack when their labels no longer fit (HIG, Typography).

## iPhone Duo

Apple's foldable iPhone, on sale from 2026-10-23. Checked on 2026-10-24 against Apple's HIG page, developer article and "Prepare" page for iPhone Duo, the UIKit references named below and five Apple tech talks on the device (`docs/sources.md`). Check an adaptation of an existing app against every bullet, its own bars, sheets and overlays first.

- **Displays.** Each display has a front camera. The outer display is wider and shorter than other iPhones' (App Store screenshots 1398 × 2034 px), so a layout there gets less height. Size classes: outer, compact width with regular height in portrait and compact height in landscape; inner, regular width and height in both orientations. The outer display honours the app's supported orientations; the inner one does not, so design each screen for both. Built with the iOS 26 SDK or earlier, an app shows inside as a centred window with empty space around it; with the iOS 27 SDK it fills most of the display; with 27.1, all of it, bars vertical.
- **Size class, not pose.** Choose a layout from the size class and from the shape of the container, wider than tall or taller than wide; never from the device, the interface orientation, the screen or a list of known sizes. Regular width does not mean iPad. The compact-width layout (outer) and the regular-width layout (inner) are the basis for every pose. Inside, extend the iPad layout where the app has one; otherwise give the space structure instead of stretching the phone layout: a split view with one more level of hierarchy, one column that becomes two, or a tab bar that becomes a sidebar for an information-dense app. Function, hierarchy and state stay the same on both displays. Write sizes as rules of the container (columns, ratios, minimum and maximum widths) with full-screen numbers only as examples. The one pose worth its own layout is tabletop, half open in inner portrait: media above, controls below, the same controls as in every other pose.
- **Other window sizes.** Every app takes part in Split View on the inner display, 50/50, each app with its bar column on its own outer edge (the left app's on the left). A Picture in Picture video pinned to the top shortens the window while it plays; iPhone Mirroring on a Mac resizes it freely. Cover these: a Split View half takes the layout of the size class it reports (at half the inner width, expect compact; check it in Device Hub), and a shorter window keeps every control reachable.
- **Bars.** On the outer display, and on the inner display in landscape, the navigation bar, toolbar and tab bar share one vertical column on the outer camera's edge (trailing in portrait), holding from the top the Dynamic Island, the status bar, the toolbar and the tab bar; it keeps that side in right-to-left languages. Inner portrait keeps top and bottom bars. Only bars owned by system navigation containers move into the column (a navigation stack's bars, a tab view's tab bar, a split view's detail column); a bar or header the app draws stays put (see System containers). Order in the column: Back or Close, then the prominent action such as Done, then the other items in their groups. An item with a symbol goes vertical; a title-only item, a segmented control or a custom view stays horizontal, so give each item a symbol and a title (the title shows in the overflow menu). Items overflow bottom-up into the system overflow menu; give frequent and badged items a high visibility priority. A navigation-focused view compresses the toolbar first (the default), a task-focused view minimises the tab bar instead. A Live Activity grows the Dynamic Island down the column, and bar items yield to the overflow menu. Reduce Transparency gives the vertical bar a solid background. A screen without bars that is immersive and does not scroll may use the full width if nothing interactive meets the Dynamic Island or the status bar; a full-width background may sit behind inset scrollable content.
- **Sheets, alerts and menus.** Outside, a system sheet puts its toolbar in the column (turn it off for a control-heavy sheet with one button); inside, a sheet sits centred with horizontal bars, and one placed on the trailing side gets a vertical bar. Half open, system sheets, alerts, action sheets, context menus, popovers and toolbar buttons move off the fold by themselves; an app-drawn sheet or overlay does not, so specify a system presentation wherever one fits, with the custom content inside it.
- **The fold and the cameras.** Interactive elements stay clear of the outer camera, the inner camera while active, and the fold while half open; scrollable content (a feed, an article, a list) may cross the fold and stays put. Inside, the fold runs horizontally in portrait and vertically in landscape, like a book. Half open in portrait the top bar stays above the fold and the app's own controls go below it; half open like a book, an alert moves to the trailing side. System containers handle the fold (a split view evens out to 50/50). A custom view that places its own controls (a floating control, an overlay, a keypad, a custom player) takes the fold from the reserved regions (`UIView.ReservedRegion`, iOS 27.1: a division, active only while half open, readable while inactive so the layout does not jump), not from the middle of the window; two related views side by side or stacked use an arrangement view (`UIArrangementViewController`: split for main and detail, overlay for controls over media). Move only what is needed, in small steps, and give a grid an even number of columns.
- **Edges and media.** Safe areas and layout margins are asymmetric: handle each edge alone; content may sit closer to a vertical bar than to the opposite edge. Full-bleed media set to fill loses content on wider displays: choose fill or fit by size class or aspect ratio, or set a focal point. A hero image under a vertical bar extends with `UIBackgroundExtensionView`, the image itself in the safe area. A custom element near a screen corner follows the corner (iOS 26 concentricity API).
- **Checks.** Device Hub (Xcode 27.1) opens, closes, rotates and folds the simulator; drag the app by the home indicator to a side for Split View. Name what the design needs checked there: both displays in both orientations, half open, both Split View sides.

## Android 16 and Material 3 Expressive

- **Android 16 (API 36)** has been required for Play updates since 2026-08-31.
- **Edge-to-edge** has no opt-out. Handle the system bar insets.
- **Large screens.** For an app that targets API 36, on a display whose smallest width is at least 600 dp the system ignores orientation, resizability and aspect-ratio restrictions. Games and a person's own choice in the aspect-ratio settings are exempt. Targeting API 37 removes the developer opt-out. A book-style foldable's inner display is such a display; its outer display and a flip phone's cover screen are below 600 dp.
- **Predictive back.** For an app targeting API 36 the animations are on by default, `onBackPressed` is not called and `KEYCODE_BACK` is not dispatched. Intercept Back with the back callbacks (`OnBackPressedCallback`, or `BackHandler` and `PredictiveBackHandler` in Compose), including for an unsaved-changes prompt.
- **Approximate location.** Since Android 12 a person can grant approximate location only, whatever the app asks for. It is accurate to about 3 km², where precise is usually within about 50 m (`ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION`, `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION`). Design the approximate case: it does not find a street or the nearest stop.
- **Windows.** Desktop windowing joins split-screen and freeform windows. Any of them can hand the app a compact width at any moment.
- **Navigation (Material 3 Expressive, May 2025).** The navigation drawer is deprecated in favour of the expanded navigation rail (220 to 360 dp). The collapsed rail is 96 dp. The navigation bar is 64 dp. Top app bars are 64 dp small, 112 dp medium and 152 dp large.
- **FAB (Material 3 Expressive).** 56 dp, medium 80 dp, large 96 dp. The 40 dp small FAB survives only as a baseline variant and is no longer recommended. Extended FAB: small 56 dp, medium 80 dp, large 96 dp.
- **Motion (Material 3 Expressive).** Components animate with spring tokens under one theme-level scheme: expressive, the recommended default, which overshoots, or standard, with minimal bounce, for utilitarian products. Each scheme has spatial tokens (position, size, rotation, corners) and effects tokens (colour and opacity, which never overshoot), each at fast, default and slow, for example `md.sys.motion.spring.fast.spatial`. In Compose: `MotionScheme.expressive()` and `MotionScheme.standard()`, read from `MaterialTheme.motionScheme`. Transitions between screens have not moved to springs and are still specified with easing and duration tokens.
- **Foldables.** Tabletop is half-opened with a horizontal hinge, book is half-opened with a vertical hinge. Keep content and controls off a hinge that separates or occludes the display (`FoldingFeature.isSeparating`, `occlusionType`). Treat a posture change like a width change: state survives it.

## Width, on both platforms

| Width class | Window width | Layout | Android navigation | iPadOS navigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Compact | under 600 dp, or a compact size class | one pane | navigation bar | tab bar at the bottom |
| Medium | 600 to 839 dp | one pane, or two if the detail pane keeps at least 320 dp | navigation rail, 96 dp | system tab bar near the top |
| Expanded | 840 to 1199 dp | two panes | expanded navigation rail, 220 to 360 dp | the same tab bar, or a sidebar |
| Large | 1200 to 1599 dp | two panes | expanded navigation rail | tab bar or sidebar |
| Extra-large | 1600 dp and wider | two panes, or three | expanded navigation rail | tab bar or sidebar |

- Large and extra-large are Android's newer width classes (Jetpack WindowManager 1.5.0; in Compose `material3-adaptive` 1.3.0, `currentWindowAdaptiveInfoV2()`, which replaces the deprecated `currentWindowAdaptiveInfo`). Height classes: compact under 480 dp, medium 480 to 899 dp, expanded 900 dp and taller.
- Material 3 recommends two panes through large and suggests a third only at extra-large. A third pane in a narrower window is a deliberate choice: show the arithmetic that every pane keeps its minimum width.
- Slide Over and a narrow Split View return an iPad to compact width while the app is running, so the compact layout is never optional on a tablet.
- Choose from the established large-screen layouts before inventing one: list-detail, supporting pane, feed.
- A width change keeps scroll position, selection, text being typed and open sheets.
- A detail pane has its own empty state. Back is defined both with two panes and after they collapse into one.
- Touch minimums do not shrink for a pointer. Hover adds and is never the only path. Every drag has a path that is not a drag.
- Wider windows get more columns or wider margins, never longer lines: a reading column stays at 45 to 75 characters, about 640 to 720 pt.
